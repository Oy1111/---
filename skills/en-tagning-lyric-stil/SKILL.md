# Én tagning (continuous one-take) style for TikTok lyric edits

Use together with the viral-lyric-edit skill (timing, captions, rules about brands/people/weapons, outro and versioning all come from there). This file only describes the look the user wants when they say 'som den her video', 'den skifter ikke scener', 'det er som om det er en hel by', 'han går ind i butikken', 'gør så der ikke er sceneskift', 'kameraet må kun bevæge sig efter teksten'. The user's standard: every new edit in this style must be MUCH more advanced than a simple flat drawing - every phrase and word in the lyrics gets its own payoff in the world, and the whole thing must look like the reference videos.

This style can also be picked when the user asks for a 'ny stil' / 'ny still' version of a song and it has not been used for that song yet.

## What the user means by 'ingen sceneskift' (learned on Beko x Marco - Tiden, Download_19 reference)
- ONE location, ONE world, and ONLY the camera moves - following the lyrics: pans, glides, push-ins onto a detail (a compass in a hand, a thermometer on the wall, the door, family photos, a mirror, the window sill), pull-backs to a wide shot. This is what the nat-streg reference (Download_19) does: one office, the camera pushes into the glass/bottle, cuts close to the shoes, etc.
- NOT acceptable (the user rejected these): hard cuts between separate scenes; 'portal' circle wipes where the next scene opens in a circle; a building/dollhouse where every lyric line sits in its own window and the camera hops window to window ('den skifter bare sceener stadig').
- So instead of one scene per line, put every lyric payoff as an object or event INSIDE the same room/place: e.g. Tiden = a night living room with a clock tower visible through the big window (intro starts tight on the tower clock in the window and pulls back at midnight to reveal the room; the girl in a coral dress by the window; boys around her; the hero holding a compass; thermometer on the wall; the front door with a boy holding flowers; family photo frames; a mirror; mugs on the sill while the window sky turns to dawn; a shooting star in the window; the pendant lamp becoming a spotlight; at the end the camera dives into the tower clock in the window which cracks and shows the title).
- Time-of-day or lighting changes (dawn in the window, room dimming to one spotlight) and objects appearing are fine - they happen in the same world, not as new scenes.

- Semi-realistic flat illustration with soft lighting: warm sodium/amber window light vs deep navy/green shadows, glow halos around lamps, bokeh, film grain, vignette, figures as dark silhouettes with a warm rim light, rich textures (cobblestones, wallpaper, felt, paper).
- Each word/phrase has a literal payoff in the world exactly on the word (a door opening, a poster, chips on the table, a lighter igniting the photo, notes fanning out).

- Outro: artist name small letter-spaced, then LIKE / FØLG / SKRIV rows.

## How to build it (worked for Carmon - Sunset, Merro8 - Hele Dit Hold, Beko x Marco - Tiden)
- One big world in world units (e.g. 4300 x 3500: a row of buildings, a cross-street gap to the horizon, sidewalk, road; or 3000 x 4600: two tower blocks, a gable, a courtyard court; or a single room ~3000 x 1920 with door, wall frames, mirror, big window, lamp, people) drawn in vector every frame; the camera is a keyframe list (time, cx, cy, zoom) with eased interpolation (a shot(fx, fy, z, screen_y) helper that puts a world point at a chosen screen y below the caption block is handy), plus a tiny handheld drift, zoom interpolated in log space; for big zoom changes the centre uses w = (1/z0 - 1/z) / (1/z0 - 1/z1) so it heads straight into the target. Start wide or start tight on a key object and pull back on the first sung word.
- Hold each push-in until its key-word event has played; start the glide to the next detail ~0.3-0.4 s before the next line; a whip-pan (0.3 s) is fine for a big jump across the room. Make events that the camera arrives at late (a door that should be seen open) start a beat earlier or key them to a later word of the line.
- Entering a shop/window without a cut: draw the interior as a full inner canvas inside the door/window rect (translate(rect corner) scale(rect_h/1920), clipped); the rect aspect must be 0.5625 so that at zoom ZIN = 1920/rect_h the interior fills the screen exactly. Use this only as part of one journey (street -> into a shop), never as a way to stack many separate scenes.
- A distant landmark seen through the window (e.g. a clock tower) can be drawn by the same function as a full-size object, scaled ~0.6 inside the clipped window, so the camera can dive into it at the end.
- Silhouette rim light that looks right: push_group, fill the body path in the rim colour, then with OPERATOR_ATOP fill the same path shifted (-side * ~1.6% of the height, +half that) in near-black, pop and paint - this leaves a thin rim on the outer outline only. Stroking the path instead shows seams between the overlapping limb/torso sub-paths. A hood is one rounded shape (a hood + a separate head circle looks like a helmet); long hair = a narrow rounded shape behind the head down to the shoulders.
- Keep in-world displays (scoreboard, signs) below the caption block: with the captions in y 250-650, put the camera so those displays land at screen y > 650. A mirror reflection needs a lighter mirror background so the dark reflection reads, and must not sit right behind the real person's head.
- Time of day can run through the edit (golden hour -> sunset -> dusk -> night, or night -> dawn in a window -> night): sky gradient stops, sun height, facade tint, windows and street lamps switching on in a chain on key words / intro beats, stars and moon at the end.
- Characters: original silhouettes (tapered legs, shoulders, hood/cap or long hair, a coloured dress as the one accent), ~350-700 world px tall and camera zoom ~1.0-1.6 on them so they read on a phone; a recurring prop (e.g. a bouquet, a ball, a deck of cards, a compass) carries the story; rivals can be the same silhouettes with a cold blue rim light instead of amber.
- Finish every frame with a low-alpha navy haze or light rain, a dark gradient band behind the captions, cycled grain and a radial vignette.
- In-world title at the end (a rooftop neon sign, the court scoreboard, the title appearing on a cracked clock face), held ~1 s before the outro card; the outro overlay must be ~0.95 dark so the in-world title doesn't ghost through.
- Still apply the viral-lyric-edit rule: a completely new world, story and key-word treatment every time - only the one-take camera grammar and the look stay.
